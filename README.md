# Skills-Design
Design knowledge you can install, not just read.

Three skills built from real UX practice at Mila packaged so engineers, researchers, students, and designers can use them directly in their coding environment through Claude.

## What's inside

### talk-to-users
Helps you prepare before you talk to anyone. 
→ Covers the front end of user research: sharpening a hypothesis into something falsifiable, picking the right method given your actual constraints (time, budget, access), writing unbiased interview and survey questions, and assembling a full interview brief. Includes a bias audit mode where you paste your own draft questions and get back what's wrong with them and why, not just a silent rewrite. 
→ Bilingual FR/EN. 
→ Comes with an annotated link library of curated UX research resources.
→ Four modes: method selection under constraints, question bank, bias audit, interview brief.

### brainstorm-with-friction
A structured brainstorming protocol designed to slow you down before you produce. Four steps: mode check (diverge or converge?), a batch of opening questions that surface hidden assumptions, a ping-pong dialogue with real pushback, and a synthesis gate that blocks any deliverable until you explicitly ask for one. 
→ The point is to think better, not to write faster. 
→ Bilingual FR/EN.

### critical-look
Generative friction on demand. You bring a reasoning, a decision, a draft, a reaction, or a disagreement. The skill challenges it (2 to 3 angles), opens it (framings you missed, the stronger version of your own idea), and leaves you with two questions to sit with. 
→ It does not rewrite, does not decide, does not deliver a finished thought. You remain the one who thinks. 
→ Bilingual FR/EN.

## What you get

Each .skill file is a self-contained package you install in Claude. Once active, the skill triggers automatically when your prompt matches its scope. No setup, no config, no API key.
W
hat's inside each package: a SKILL.md (the instructions Claude follows) and, for some skills, a references/ folder with operational content like question banks, bias taxonomies, method-selection matrices, and link libraries.


## Why this exists
Most design knowledge lives in decks, Notion pages, and books that people read once and forget. These skills put that knowledge where the work happens: in the conversation with the AI, at the moment a decision is being made.

They come from a specific belief: good questions come before good products. The cost of skipping the thinking phase does not show up immediately. It shows up later, in the wrong feature built for the wrong reason, validated by the wrong question.

These skills are the thinking phase, packaged.

## What this is not
→ These are not general-purpose AI prompts or "awesome prompt" collections. They are opinionated, structured protocols built from practice. They will push back on you. They will ask you to think before producing. If you want Claude to just write something fast, these are not for you.
→ They also do not cover the full UX research lifecycle. talk-to-users handles the front end (framing, method choice, questions, briefs), not synthesis, recruitment operations, or statistical analysis.
